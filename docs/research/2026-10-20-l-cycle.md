# The next cycle: the other direction, and the phone's window (2026-10-20, L)

Date: 2026-10-20, after the K cycle (`docs/research/2026-10-20-k-cycle.md`). K1 (file dates), K2 (pause and mute one app), K3 (notification icons and pictures), K4 (volume keys) and K7 (media controls) are merged; K5 (the phone's sound with its screen) is in review; K6 (a browser link) is deferred, since it opens a network listener and a firewall prompt; K8 (copies sent by themselves) waits for its spike on the phone. Nothing below repeats them.

## Where we are

- **The phone reaches the computer for nearly everything:** sessions, the remote, the deck, files, the clipboard, notifications with answers, texts, calls, the camera, widgets and tiles.
- **Gaps the repo shows, each checked against the code for this plan:**
  - **A notification cleared on the computer stays on the phone.** The phone → computer direction exists (N5c part 1). The phone never calls `cancelNotification`, and nothing asks it to.
  - **The phone's cast window has no file drop and no paste.** `take_files_to_send` reads drops on the main window only. `cast_view.rs` turns `Event::Text` into typing but ignores `Event::Paste`.
  - **The cast window cannot save a screenshot or record.**
  - **The battery warns only when low** (`phone_battery_alerts`, 15 %). Nothing says a phone has charged.
  - **The computer cannot start a call.** No `ACTION_DIAL` anywhere, and no `tel:` handler.
  - **The computer cannot reach the phone's automation apps.** The phone's broadcasts (1f) carry only the phone's own events.
  - **One phone feature needs switches on both sides,** and neither side shows the other's. A new pairing gets the default permissions with no choice.
  - **Background features go to one computer.** `PhoneLink.target` is the first paired saved computer: notifications, texts, calls and the battery reach only that one.
  - **The phone's own state on the computer is the battery only.**

The owner's standing rules hold:
- spec, then TDD; one logical change per commit;
- a fresh review per item, its findings fixed one commit each;
- gates per change; device checks batched for the owner;
- docs with each change; knobs in Settings on both sides;
- no macOS or iOS;
- no power, lock or server-stop actions on the owner's computer; no driver installs.

## L1. Cleared on the computer, cleared on the phone

**What:** a phone notification closed on the computer is cleared on the phone:
- the toast closed with its ✕ (`ToastNotification.Dismissed`, `UserCanceled`, while the server lives);
- Clear on the Notifications page;
- a Linux notification closed by the user (`NotificationClosed`, reason 2).

The server sends KDE's own `kdeconnect.notification.request {cancel: id}`. The phone calls `cancelNotification(key)` for a notification it sent to that computer, and only one that is clearable. The same works for KDE devices (N9), so a KDE phone clears too.

Settings:
- the computer: "Clearing here clears it on the phone", on;
- the phone: "Let the computer clear my notifications", on once notifications are on.

**Why:** KDE bug 400595, the 2024–2025 forum thread, the AMA.

**Touches:**
- **The phone:** the listener keeps a map from the id it sent to the notification's key. Clearing is honoured only from its own computer, at most 30 a minute.
- **The computer:** the toast's `Dismissed` handler beside N3's `Activated`; the D-Bus listener; the Notifications page's Clear.

**Checked here:**
- JVM tests: which ids are cleared, ongoing ones refused, another computer refused;
- server tests for the packet;
- the throwaway chat app: a notification posted, cleared through the page's request file, gone from `adb shell dumpsys notification --noredact`.

**Owed:** the toast's ✕ by hand; Linux.

**Risks:**
- An Action Center "clear all" raises no event without a package identity (C9 was skipped). Say so in Settings.
- Clearing a call (F5) must not decline it: calls are left out.
- Clearing on one computer when L8 sends to several: clear on all.

## L2. Drop files and paste into the phone's window

**What:**
- **Files or folders dropped on the cast window** go to that phone, as the Files page sends them, with a line in the window's bar ("Sending 2 files…").
- **Ctrl+V in the cast window, with control on (C8),** types the computer's clipboard text at the phone's focused field. An image on the clipboard goes as N4's clipboard image instead.

**Why:** Phone Link's drag and drop; scrcpy's drop and Ctrl+V.

**Touches:** the computer only:
- `cast_view.rs` reads its own viewport's `dropped_files` and `Event::Paste`;
- the outbox and the C8 text road are as they are.

**Checked here:**
- a model test for the drop's target, and a harness test if kittest takes drops on a deferred viewport (else the model alone);
- a cast from the OPPO with the C8 test client as the window;
- a 3 MB file's MD5 over adb;
- pasted text read back from a scratch field with `uiautomator dump`.

**Risks:**
- A password manager's private copy: N2's rule holds, and nothing marked sensitive is pasted.
- Pasting long text: cap it at 4 KB, as typing is.
- A drop with Files off for that phone: say why in the bar.

## L3. "Charged to 80 %"

**What:** desktop Settings "Tell me when a phone has charged to": off, 80, 90 or 100 %. One notification per charge, reset once it is unplugged or 5 % under. This covers paired phones and KDE devices (3c), from the battery reports that already arrive.

**Why:** GSConnect has it; KDE Connect users ask for it.

**Touches:** the computer only, beside the low-battery warning (T8b).

**Checked here:**
- unit tests for the once-per-charge rule;
- `adb shell dumpsys battery set ac 1`, then levels 79 → 80 with Share battery on, and the toast read back from the Action Center as N3 did;
- `dumpsys battery reset` after.

**Risks:**
- The phone reports on change and every 5 minutes, so a level may be skipped. Fire at or above the level.
- A phone with Android's own charge limit (80 %) never reaches 100: the Settings line says so.

## L4. Call a number from the computer

**What:** "Call" beside a number on the Messages page and on a call's note (F5), and `pocketdesk call <phone> <number>`. On Windows the server can be offered as a `tel:` handler (RegisteredApplications); the owner picks it in Default apps, which Windows requires. On Linux it is an `x-scheme-handler/tel` desktop file.

The phone opens its dialer with the number (`ACTION_DIAL`, never `ACTION_CALL`). In front, it opens at once. Otherwise it shows a notification, "Call +1 555 0100 from the computer", whose tap opens the dialer (background starts are blocked).

Settings:
- the phone: "Calls from the computer", off;
- the computer: a per-phone Calls permission.

**Why:** KDE bugs 341950 and 397333; Digital Trends' Continuity gap.

**Touches:** both, with a new packet `kdeconnect.pocketdesk.dial {number}` (digits, `+`, `*`, `#`, at most 32).

**Checked here:**
- JVM and server tests for the number rule;
- without a SIM, `ACTION_DIAL` still opens the dialer: `adb shell dumpsys activity activities` shows it with the number, and the notification with `dumpsys notification`.

**Owed:** a real call (a SIM); the Default apps pick by hand.

**Risks:**
- Spoofed or premium numbers: the dialer always waits for the owner's tap.
- The `tel:` registration writes HKCU, as the Send To menu does. It is undone by `uninstall` and offered only from Settings.

## L5. Screenshot and record the phone's window

**What:** two buttons in the cast window's bar:
- **Screenshot:** the frame shown, saved as PNG in Pictures\Pocketdesk and put on the clipboard.
- **Record / Stop:** the H.264 the relay hands the viewer is copied, not re-encoded, into an MKV by an `ffmpeg -c copy` sidecar with wall-clock timestamps. It is MKV so a crash leaves a playable file.

Desktop Settings sets the folder. When K5 lands, the phone's sound is muxed in.

**Why:** scrcpy's `--record` and its GUIs' buttons.

**Touches:** the computer only (`cast_view.rs` and the ffmpeg sidecar it already runs).

**Checked here:**
- a cast from the OPPO showing a fixed screen;
- `ffprobe` on the recording: its duration within 10 % of the wall clock, codec h264, the phone's size;
- the PNG's size and a sampled pixel matching `adb exec-out screencap`.

**Risks:**
- The cast is variable frame rate and starts on a keyframe. Recording asks for a keyframe (the cast already can), or the file opens grey.
- Disk filling: show the size as it grows, and stop at a free-space floor.
- Recording a phone's private screen (FLAG_SECURE shows black, as Android sends it): say so.

## L6. Commands to the phone's automation apps

**What:** `pocketdesk phone-command <phone> <name> [text]`, the local API, and Home Assistant's button. The phone broadcasts `dev.pocketdesk.android.event.COMMAND` with `name` and `text` for Tasker, Automate or MacroDroid. This is the other half of 1f, where the computer could already hear the phone.

Settings on the phone:
- "Commands from the computer to my automation apps", off;
- "Only to", an app chosen from the installed ones (Tasker by default when present). With it set, the broadcast is sent to that package only, so no other app reads it.

**Why:**
- KDE's "Intent Listener" request and bug 377850;
- Join and AutoRemote's reason to exist.

**Touches:** both, with a packet `kdeconnect.pocketdesk.command`:
- `name`: printable, at most 64 characters;
- `text`: at most 4 KB;
- 30 a minute;
- only from its own computer.

**Checked here:**
- JVM tests for the rule;
- a throwaway receiver APK that logs what it gets, read with `adb logcat`;
- with "Only to" set elsewhere, nothing arrives.

**Owed:** Tasker itself.

**Risks:**
- Any app may receive it unless "Only to" is set: say so (SECURITY.md, as limit 20 does).
- Android 14's implicit broadcast limits: manifest receivers need an explicit package, so "Only to" is also what makes it work for most apps. Measure it on the OPPO.

## L7. Both sides' switches in one place, and presets at pairing

**What:**
- **On the computer:** for each phone, the Devices page shows one table, a row per feature with three columns:
  - "This computer allows" (the permission);
  - "The phone sends" (its own switch);
  - "Android allows" (its permission or access).

  A blocked feature says which side to change.
- **On the phone:** each computer's menu gets the mirror: what the computer allows it.
- **At pairing:** the Pair page asks "Whose phone is it?":
  - "Mine": as now;
  - "Someone else's": files and links only, no input, clipboard, notifications or texts;
  - "A guest": I3's guest pairing.

**Why:** KDE's forum, 2026-10-17 and 2026-07-20.

**Touches:** both:
- the phone sends its switches (booleans only) to its own computer, as it sends the battery;
- the computer answers with the permissions it gives that phone.

**Checked here:**
- unit tests for the table's model;
- the C4 harness for the Devices page and the presets;
- the phone's switches changed by adb (`run-as` prefs, then a restart: a prefs file written while the app runs is not read), and seen on the computer within a report.

**Risks:**
- Telling the computer what the phone has on is new information: booleans only, never package lists or folder names.
- A table too wide at the minimum width: E5's wrapping rows.

## L8. Every computer of mine

**What:** the phone keeps a background link to each paired computer it can reach, not only the first. Notifications, texts, calls, the battery and screenshots (T10h) go to each.
- **An answer or clear on one** (with L1) clears the others.
- **Quiet:** a computer in use gets the toast; the others keep it on their Notifications page only.
- **Per computer:** Settings "Send here", on by default for the first computer and off for later ones. The owner chooses.

**Why:** Phone Link's one live PC at a time; KDE users with two computers.

**Touches:** both:
- **The phone:** `PhoneLink.target` becomes a list; the link keeper keeps several links, each with its own backoff (N7's budget measured again).
- **The computer:** it reports its idle seconds (D9's `GetLastInputInfo`) on the link.

**Checked here:**
- two throwaway servers on this box (R6a's `POCKETDESK_CONFIG_DIR`, different ports), both paired with the OPPO;
- the throwaway chat app's notification arriving at both;
- a clear on one gone from the other;
- the background cost measured again by N7's script for an hour.

**Risks:**
- Battery: two links cost more. Measure, and keep links only to computers on this network or reachable from away.
- The clipboard sent to every computer leaks to one that is not the owner's: off by default for the second, and named per computer.
- A larger change to the busiest code on the phone: last in the cycle, with its own review.

## L9. The phone's state on the computer

**What:** beside the battery on the Overview and in `devices --json`:
- free storage;
- the network (Wi-Fi or mobile; never the network's name);
- the signal bars when it has service;
- the ringer mode.

It is reported with the battery, under "Share battery", renamed "Share the phone's state". Optionally, a warning when storage is under 1 GB.

**Why:** KDE Connect's connectivity report; the reverse of F2.

**Touches:** both (one more field set on `kdeconnect.battery`'s report; a KDE device's `connectivity_report` read too).

**Checked here:**
- JVM tests;
- `adb shell svc wifi disable` / `enable` and a large file pushed to fill storage, seen on the computer;
- with no SIM, the signal row says "no service".

**Risks:**
- Signal strength needs `READ_PHONE_STATE`, which F5 asks for when calls are on. Without it, no bars, and no new ask.

## Considered and left out

Quick Share receiving (GPL-3.0 reference code, needs a BLE advert, Google ships it for Windows: https://github.com/Martichou/rquickshare); call audio on the computer (Android lets no app capture it); RCS (no API); message history (`READ_SMS` and Play policy, no SIM); a Windows second screen (a driver, C12); Action Center clears and Windows notifications to the phone (package identity, C9); scrcpy's screen-off mirroring (shell rights); Wear OS (no watch).
