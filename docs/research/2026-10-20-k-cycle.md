# The next cycle: quieter, truer, and the phone's sound (2026-10-20, K)

Date: 2026-10-20, after the 2026-10-20 cycle (`docs/research/2026-10-20-next-cycle.md`) and its follow-ups G1–G7, H1–H4 and I1–I3. F1–F6 part 1 are done. F6 part 2 (a virtual webcam) waits for the owner's OK.

## Where we are

- **The phone reaches the computer for nearly everything** people ask of these apps: sessions, the remote, the deck, files both ways, the clipboard, notifications with answers, texts, calls, the camera, the computer's state, widgets and tiles.
- **680 owed checks are open** (`docs/owed-checks.md`); 391 can run here. The F–I cycles added about 15 checklist sections, most of them owed on a device.
- **Gaps the repo shows, each checked against the code for this plan:**
  - **Files lose their dates.** Neither the share, the offer, the folder sends nor the KDE bridge carries a modified time; every received file is dated when it landed.
  - **A phone notification is its title and text only.** No app icon, no contact photo, no picture (`PhoneNotifications.kt` sends `title`, `text`, `time`).
  - **The phone's volume keys do nothing for the computer.** Remote has on-screen Vol − and Vol +; the hardware keys change the phone's own volume.
  - **The computer cannot be paused**, and it cannot mute one phone app. The only per-app choice is the phone's opt-in list.
  - **The phone's sound never reaches the computer.** The cast is the screen only; the computer→phone direction (N5) and the phone's mic (E2) exist.
  - **The phone's music is controlled only from the Sessions tab**, not from the computer's own media controls or keys.
  - **Copying on the phone still needs a tap** (the tile, "To computer", the share sheet).
  - **No one without the app can get a file**: a guest pairing still needs Pocketdesk on the phone.

The owner's standing rules hold:
- spec, then TDD; one logical change per commit;
- a fresh review per item, its findings fixed one commit each;
- gates per change; device checks batched for the owner;
- docs with each change; knobs in Settings on both sides;
- no macOS or iOS;
- no power, lock or server-stop actions on the owner's computer; no virtual webcam without the owner's OK.

## K1. Files keep their dates

**What:** a file's modified time travels with it and is set where it lands, both ways:
- phone → computer shares and folders;
- computer → phone offers and folder sends;
- files from KDE devices (KDE's share packet carries `lastModified`), and to them later.

Settings "Keep files' dates" on both sides, on by default.

**Why:** KDE bug 368013/503297 (open since 2016, touched 2026-07-29); LocalSend #650 (reopened 2026-09-30).

**Touches:** both.
- **The wire:** an optional `lastModified` (ms) on the share and the offer, and on each folder entry. An older end ignores it.
- **The computer:** sets it after the rename into Downloads (`filetime` or `SetFileTime` / `utimensat`). It is never set before the file is whole.
- **The phone:**
  - **MediaStore Downloads:** `DATE_MODIFIED` is set by the provider and not reliably writable (LocalSend's maintainer said as much). Try `File.setLastModified` on the row's path where the app owns it.
  - **SAF folders (C2):** `DocumentsContract` has no setter.

  Say per place what is kept.

**Checked here:**
- unit tests on both ends;
- a file with an old date (`touch -d` on the phone by adb, `SetFileTime` here) sent each way, compared with `stat -c %Y` over adb and the computer's file time;
- a KDE device's `lastModified` in the bridge's tests.

**Risks:**
- Android may refuse the date in Downloads; then the item is partly done, and the Settings line says where dates are kept.
- A future date or garbage: clamp to now.
- A resumed transfer (T6a) must keep the first attempt's date.

## K2. Pause, and mute one app, on the computer

**What:**
- **Pause for 1 hour, until tomorrow, or until resumed:** from the tray, Settings and `pocketdesk pause`. While paused, phone notifications, calls and texts are kept on the Notifications page but not shown, links are not opened, rings and the siren are held, and the clipboard does not cross. Sessions still work: the owner chose to open one.
- **"Not from this app":** on a phone notification's row on the Notifications page and as a toast button. It mutes one phone app on this computer, listed in Settings with Unmute. The phone's own opt-in list is left alone.
- **On the phone:** its card says "Paused on the computer until 15:00".

**Why:**
- KDE's forum, 2026-10-11: pause during a screen share without turning features off.
- 2026-08-26 and 2026-08-20: one mail shown twice; per-app filters wanted on the computer.

**Touches:** the computer (server, tray, app); the phone only for the card's line.

**Checked here:**
- server unit tests (what a pause holds and what it lets through);
- the C4 harness for Settings and the Notifications page;
- the throwaway chat app on the phone: no toast while paused or muted, the toast again after Resume.

**Risks:**
- A pause forgotten for good: it always shows in the tray's icon and menu, and "until resumed" is not the default.
- What a pause holds must be one table, tested, or each feature drifts.
- Rings: a phone that asks the computer to ring while paused should be told "paused", not "rang".

## K3. Notifications with the app's icon and picture

**What:**
- **Each forwarded notification carries its app's icon**, a small PNG (at most 96 px), sent once per app and kept by hash.
- **With "Pictures in notifications" on** (off by default), it also carries its large icon (a contact's photo) and a BigPictureStyle picture, scaled to at most 720 px.
- **Where it shows:**
  - Windows: the toast's app logo and inline image;
  - Linux: `image-data`;
  - the Notifications page: the icon beside each row.
- KDE devices' notification icons (N9) the same way.

**Why:** KDE forwards them (bug 341898); Phone Link shows them; Pocketdesk's toasts are text only.

**Touches:** both.
- **The phone:** `Notification.getSmallIcon` and `getLargeIcon` drawn to a bitmap. The lock-screen privacy and one-time-code rules (N5a) also cover pictures: a hidden notification sends no picture.
- **The computer:** decodes under C7's and N4's limits (dimensions before decode). Images are kept owner-only in the state folder and removed with the notification.

**Checked here:**
- JVM tests for what is sent when;
- server tests for decode limits;
- the throwaway chat app posting a notification with a large icon and a picture on the phone, the toast read back from the Action Center (as N3 did).

**Risks:**
- Size on a slow link: icons once per app, pictures capped and dropped first under the flood guard.
- A picture can hold a code or a private photo: off by default, and it follows the same rules as the text.
- Decode bombs: dimensions checked before decoding.

## K4. The phone's volume keys for the computer

**What:** while Remote or a sound session (N5) is open, even with the screen off, the phone's volume keys change the computer's volume, and Android's volume panel names the computer.

This is done with a `MediaSession` whose playback is remote (`VolumeProvider`), as casting apps do. It is set by the existing audio volume request (Audio permission). Settings "Volume keys change the computer's volume", on by default.

**Why:** KDE bug 398848; KDE Connect 1.35.10's switch.

**Touches:** the phone; no computer change.

**Checked here:**
- JVM tests for the step and the clamp;
- `adb shell input keyevent 24/25` with Remote open against the throwaway server, its volume requests seen in the server log. The volume itself is set through a recording seam, not the owner's speakers; one real step and back needs the owner's OK.

**Risks:**
- Taking the keys from a phone call or the phone's own music: only while a remote or sound session is in front or holds the service, never during a call.
- Android 17's background volume rules (N4): check that the remote session still gets the keys.

## K5. The phone's sound on the computer

**What:** "Play the phone's sound here", in each computer's menu and as a switch in "Show screen".

The phone captures what its apps play (`AudioPlaybackCapture`, Android 10+, under the MediaProjection consent the cast already asks for). It encodes Opus with the encoder E2's mic share uses, and sends it on the cast session or on a sound-only one.

The computer decodes and plays it on the default output, or a chosen one, through the path the mic relay uses. A per-phone permission, "Play its sound here", is off by default.

**Why:** scrcpy 2.0's audio forwarding; Phone Link's "hear audio on PC"; whole apps for it (SoundDrift, AudioRelay).

**Touches:** both.

**Checked here:**
- **The phone, by adb:** a throwaway app plays a 1 kHz tone, and the computer writes what it receives to a WAV seam (`POCKETDESK_PHONE_AUDIO_FILE`). A test checks the tone's frequency and level, and the delay from a marked start.
- **Gates:** JVM tests for the policy, and server tests for decode and the seam.
- **Owed:** by ear, and a real music app.

**Risks:**
- Apps may forbid capture (`ALLOW_CAPTURE_BY_NONE`, call audio always). Say "This app does not allow its sound to be shared" when the stream is silent while media plays.
- The phone's speaker keeps playing: whether muting it silences the capture must be measured on the OPPO first.
- Android 14 asks consent per projection.
- Latency against the cast's picture: the audio is not synced to video (roadmap 2794); measure it and say so.

## K6. A link for a browser

**What:** in the desktop app's Files page:
- **"Share by link":** choose files; the computer serves them to any browser on the local network at a one-time address, shown as a link and a QR code.
- **"Receive by link":** a page where a browser can upload files into Downloads.

Each ends after a time the owner picks (10 minutes by default) or on Stop. Each serves only the chosen files and has a random token in the path. It binds only the addresses phones use at home, never the internet road, and is off unless the owner starts it. Settings has the default time and HTTPS (self-signed, the browser warns).

**Why:** LocalSend's "Share via link" (and its HTTPS option in 1.11.0). Pocketdesk's guest pairing still needs the app.

**Touches:** the computer (server and app). It reuses D8's HTTP code, the share safety rules (names, sizes, Mark of the Web, `.part`) and C1's rows for progress.

**Checked here:**
- server tests with curl (the token, expiry, ranges, an upload's name and size checks, refusals after Stop);
- the C4 harness for the page;
- Chrome on the phone by adb (`am start -a android.intent.action.VIEW -d <link>`), the download's MD5 matched.

**Risks:**
- **A listener on the LAN.** Mitigations:
  - a fresh token;
  - a time limit;
  - a firewall rule only while it runs (the `install` rules cover the control port only);
  - rate limits;
  - a log line per fetch.
- Plain HTTP on a shared network: say it on the page, and offer HTTPS.
- Uploads from strangers: off unless "Receive" is started, with a size cap and the owner told of each file.
- Windows' firewall prompt on the first listen: name it in the docs.

## K7. The phone's music in the computer's own media controls

**What:** while a phone reports what it plays (T8d), the tray program registers it with the computer's own media controls:
- **Windows:** `SystemMediaTransportControls` through `ISystemMediaTransportControlsInterop::GetForWindow` on the tray's hidden window, so the volume flyout shows it with Play/Pause, Previous and Next.
- **Linux:** an MPRIS player on the session bus.

A press goes through `phone_media_out/` as the Sessions tab's does. Settings "The phone's music in this computer's media controls", off by default.

**Why:** KDE Connect does this on Plasma; Phone Link keeps its player in its own window.

**Touches:** the computer (the tray program and server).

**Checked here:**
- a test that reads the session back through `GlobalSystemMediaTransportControlsSessionManager` (T8c's reader), with a probe phone's report as T8d's device run used;
- a press from that reader reaching `phone_media_out/`.
- **Owed:** the keyboard's media keys (input on this box, the owner's OK), Linux.

**Risks:**
- The phone's session may take the keyboard's media keys from the owner's own player. Off by default, and the session is dropped when the phone stops playing.
- Without the tray program there is no window to hang it on: the window's own tray holds it, as E2's fallback does.

## K8. Copying on the phone sends by itself (a spike first)

**What:** with "Send copies by themselves" on, a copy on the phone goes to its computer without a tap. Two ways are known:
- **`READ_LOGS` granted by adb** (KDE's wiki): watch the log for the denied clipboard read, then read it from an invisible window.
- **The control Accessibility service (C8)** noticing a copy, then the same invisible read (XClipper's way).

**The spike, an hour by adb on the OPPO (Android 14), decides first:**
- Does `logcat` with `READ_LOGS` still work from the background? Android 13 added LogcatManager, which asks the user and is said to deny background readers; this may end the first way.
- Does the system's clipboard overlay or a copy action raise an Accessibility event the service can see?
- Does the invisible window get focus from the background (Android's background activity starts)?

If neither way works on Android 14, K8 is dropped and the plan says why.

**Why:**
- KDE's forum, 2026-09-21 to 2026-10-03: "Send Clipboard" is too tedious.
- The January 2026 clipboard thread.
- KDE's wiki keeps the adb workaround.

**Touches:** the phone; the computer as now (the session clipboard and N2's road).

**Checked here:** by adb only.
- a throwaway app copies text;
- the computer's clipboard seam (`POCKETDESK_CLIPBOARD_FILE`) receives it;
- a copy marked sensitive does not go.

**Risks:**
- It is a workaround a future Android may close.
- Widening C8's Accessibility service raises what Android 17's Advanced Protection revokes and what Play's policy reviews. It needs its own switch, off by default.
- Battery: a log reader or event filter running all day; measure with N7's script.

## Left out, and why

- **Unlock the computer with the phone** (Samsung Flow): a Windows credential provider is a security component, and testing it touches this box's lock screen, which the owner's rule forbids.
- **The virtual webcam** (F6 part 2): it registers a camera device and waits for the owner's OK.
- **The phone's hotspot from the computer** (Phone Link's Instant Hotspot, Samsung only): Android does not let an ordinary app turn tethering on.
- **Desktop notifications on the phone, on Windows:** `UserNotificationListener` needs a package identity (C9, set aside). Linux could, but not checked here.
- **Phone apps in their own windows on the computer** (Phone Link on Samsung, scrcpy's `--new-display`): launching other apps on an app's own virtual display needs shell or system rights.
- **A taskbar target for dropped files** (Microsoft's 2026 prototype): D5 found the notification area takes no drops; the taskbar needs a pinned window, and the tray program has none.
- **AV1 or 120 fps sessions** (Moonlight/Apollo): this box's RTX 3060 has no AV1 encoder and its NVENC is driver-blocked (AGENTS.md); nothing to measure.
- **Pause media during calls** (KDE's feature, behind the Pause request): calls need a SIM to check.
- **A Messages app with whole conversations** (Microsoft's prototype, KDE's SMS window): the phone here has no SIM.
- **Links opened in a chosen app by address** (KDE forum, 2026-07-27, YouTube Music in AudioTube): one request; a small rule list after K2 if more ask.
- **A writable phone drive** (KDE's KIO/remote:/ in Gear 26.08): D8 is read-only, and writes through Android's SAF from Explorer's many small requests need their own design.
- **A Bluetooth road** (KDE's 2026 Windows Bluetooth bugs): no case here that Wi-Fi, the LAN and iroh do not cover.
